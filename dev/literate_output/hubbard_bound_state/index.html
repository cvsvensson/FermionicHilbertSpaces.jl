Bound doublons in the Hubbard ring

Two fermions with opposite spin on a Hubbard ring with attractive interaction U < 0 form a bound pair, a doublon. Its energy at total momentum K is known exactly. We find it numerically with KrylovKit, acting with the symbolic Hamiltonian directly on symbolic states, without ever building a basis or a matrix for the full Hilbert space.

using FermionicHilbertSpaces, KrylovKit, LinearAlgebra, Plotsusing FermionicHilbertSpaces.NonCommutativeProducts: terms

A ring of L sites with spin has 2L fermionic modes. The Hilbert space is only described, not enumerated.

@fermions cL = 16H = hilbert_space(c, [(j, s) for j in 1:L for s in (:↑, :↓)])dim(H)
4294967296

The Hubbard Hamiltonian with periodic boundary conditions.

t, U = 1.0, -4.0ham = sum(-t * c[j, s]' * c[mod1(j + 1, L), s] + hc for j in 1:L for s in (:↑, :↓)) +      U * sum(c[j, :↑]' * c[j, :↑] * c[j, :↓]' * c[j, :↓] for j in 1:L)
Sum with 80 terms: 
-c†[1, :↑]*c[16, :↑]-c†[1, :↑]*c[2, :↑]-c†[1, :↓]*c[16, :↓] + ...

A doublon with momentum K is a superposition of a spin-up and a spin-down fermion sitting on the same site.

vac = Ket("0"^(2L), H)doublon(K) = sum(cis(K * j) * c[j, :↑]' * c[j, :↓]' * vac for j in 1:L) / sqrt(L)
doublon (generic function with 1 method)

The Hamiltonian conserves particle number and momentum, so the Krylov space generated from doublon(K) stays in the two-particle sector with momentum K. The lowest eigenvalue there is the bound state. eigsolve takes the symbolic Hamiltonian as the linear map and symbolic states as vectors. With eager=true it stops as soon as the eigenvalue has converged, before rounding errors can pull in states with other momenta.

function bound_state(K)    vals, vecs, info = eigsolve(ham, doublon(K), 1, :SR; ishermitian=true, eager=true)    return vals[1], vecs[1]endE0, ψ0 = bound_state(0.0)

The eigenvector is a sparse sum of basis states, a vanishingly small part of the full Hilbert space.

(; hilbert_dim=dim(H), support=length(collect(terms(ψ0))))
(hilbert_dim = 4294967296, support = 256)

For H = -t Σ (c†c + h.c.) + U Σ n↑n↓ the bound state energy is E(K) = -√(U² + 16t² cos²(K/2)) on an infinite chain. On the ring, K = 2πm/L, and the finite-size corrections decay exponentially with L.

Eexact(K) = -sqrt(U^2 + 16t^2 * cos(K / 2)^2)Ks = [2π * m / L for m in 0:L÷2]Es = [first(bound_state(K)) for K in Ks]maximum(abs, Es .- Eexact.(Ks))
4.24773224327879e-6

The numerical energies follow the exact dispersion across the whole band.

plot(Eexact, 0, π; label="exact", xlabel="K", ylabel="E", lw=2, frame=:box, size=(500, 300))scatter!(Ks, Es; label="eigsolve, L = $L")
