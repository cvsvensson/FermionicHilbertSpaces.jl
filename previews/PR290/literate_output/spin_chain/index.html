Spin chains

This tutorial builds spin operators and a Heisenberg chain, and restricts it to a magnetization sector and a total-spin sector. It assumes Getting started.

using FermionicHilbertSpacesusing LinearAlgebra

Spin operators

@spin T declares one spin whose components are indexed by :x, :y, :z and the ladder operators :+, :-. Without a spin value, the operators satisfy only the spin algebra.

@spin TT[:x] * T[:y] - T[:y] * T[:x] == 1im * T[:z]
true

A spin value such as 1//2 or 1 adds the corresponding normalization.

@spin Shalf 1//2@spin Sone 1Shalf[:x]^2 == 1 // 4 # truesum(op^2 for op in Sone[:]) == 2
true

For spin one half, expressions simplify to a unique form. For higher spins they do not, so equal operators can compare unequal as symbolic expressions. Compare their matrices instead:

representation(Sone[:x]^3, hilbert_space(Sone), :dense) ≈ representation(Sone[:x], hilbert_space(Sone), :dense)
true

Heisenberg chain

@spins S declares a field of spins, with S[k][:x] the operator on site k. hilbert_space(S, labels) builds the space of the sites in labels.

The chain conserves the total magnetization. An AdditiveConstraint restricts the space to states where the sum over sites of s.m is fixed:

Hm = hilbert_space(S, 1:N, AdditiveConstraint(0; maps = s -> s.m))representation(ham, Hm, :dense)

Advanced: total spin sectors

The total spin $J$ is fixed by the Casimir operator $vec S_\mathrm{tot}^2 = J(J+1)$. Projecting onto its eigenspaces block-diagonalizes the Hamiltonian, and the blocks together reproduce its spectrum.

Mham = representation(ham, Hm, :dense)Stot2 = sum(sum(S[k][op] for k in 1:N)^2 for op in (:x, :y, :z))Mtot = representation(Stot2, Hm, :dense)blocks = map(J -> nullspace(Mtot - J * (J + 1) * I), 0:2)sort(reduce(vcat, [eigvals(Hermitian(P' * Mham * P)) for P in blocks])) ≈ eigvals(Hermitian(Mham))
true

For Hamiltonians symmetric under permuting sites, symmetric_sector projects onto permutation sectors; see Spatial symmetries.
