Braiding Majorana zero modes

Exchanging two Majorana zero modes $γ_i$ and $γ_j$ acts on the degenerate ground states with the unitary

\[B_{ij} = \exp\left(\frac{π}{4} γ_i γ_j\right) = \frac{1 + γ_i γ_j}{\sqrt{2}},\]

which maps $γ_j → γ_i$ and $γ_i → -γ_j$. The second form uses $(γ_i γ_j)^2 = -1$, which holds because the package normalises Majoranas as $γ^2 = 1$. Here we braid two Majoranas adiabatically in a tri-junction, where a central Majorana $γ_0$ couples to three outer ones, and check that the time evolution reproduces $B_{32}$.

using FermionicHilbertSpacesusing LinearAlgebra, Plots@majoranas γγ[1] * γ[1]
1I

The Majoranas γ[4] and γ[5] are spectators that never couple to anything. They pair up with the two zero modes of the junction so that the ground space within one parity sector is two-dimensional. The Hamiltonian conserves parity, so it suffices to work in the even sector.

H = hilbert_space(γ, 0:5, ParityConservation(1))hs = [representation(1im * γ[0] * γ[i], H, :dense) for i in 1:3]

The couplings $Δ_i(t)$ between $γ_0$ and $γ_i$ are smooth steps, switched on and off in turn so that at least one is always on. While $γ_0$ couples to $γ_1$ only, $γ_2$ and $γ_3$ are zero modes. Over one period a zero mode moves from $γ_2$ to $γ_1$, then from $γ_3$ to $γ_2$ and finally from $γ_1$ to $γ_3$, which exchanges $γ_2$ and $γ_3$.

coupling(t, T, k) = (1 + tanh(k * cos(2π * t / T))) / 2couplings(t, T, k) = [coupling(t - (i - 1) * T / 3, T, k) for i in 1:3]hamiltonian(t, T, k) = Hermitian(sum(couplings(t, T, k) .* hs))T, k = 1000, 20ts = range(0, T, 300)plot(ts, stack(couplings.(ts, T, k))'; label=["Δ₁" "Δ₂" "Δ₃"], xlabel="t", legend=:outerright, frame=:box, size=(450, 250), lw=2)

For a slow protocol, the Schrödinger evolution can be approximated by a product of exponentials of the Hamiltonian at the midpoints of short time steps.

dt = 1.0U = foldl((U, t) -> exp(-1im * dt * hamiltonian(t, T, k)) * U, dt/2:dt:T; init=Matrix{ComplexF64}(I, dim(H), dim(H)))

The ground states at $t = 0$ and $t = T$ coincide. Restricted to them, the evolution should equal the braid operator up to the dynamical phase, which is the same for all ground states.

V = eigvecs(hamiltonian(0, T, k))[:, 1:2]B = representation((1 + γ[3] * γ[2]) / sqrt(2), H, :dense)Uground = V' * U * VBground = V' * B * Vphase = tr(Uground * Bground') / 2@assert Uground ≈ phase * Bgroundabs(phase)
1.0000000000000027

The opposite exchange $B_{23} = B_{32}^†$ is not reproduced, since it differs from $B_{32}$ by more than a phase.
