Schrieffer–Wolff transformation of the Anderson model

linsolve from KrylovKit works on symbolic kets, so second-order perturbation theory can be done exactly in a space far too large for matrices. We compute the effective Hamiltonian of an Anderson impurity coupled to 81 bath levels on its four-dimensional low-energy subspace and recover the Kondo exchange $J\,\mathbf{S}\cdot\mathbf{s}$ of Schrieffer and Wolff[1][2].

Second-order effective Hamiltonian

Let $P$ project onto a degenerate low-energy subspace of $H_0$ with energy $E_0$, and let $V$ couple it only to states outside. To second order in $V$ the dynamics within the subspace is generated by $H_\mathrm{eff} = P V (E_0 - H_0)^{-1} V P$.

For the Anderson model, the low-energy states have one electron on the impurity, one at the Fermi level and all levels below filled. Schrieffer and Wolff found that $H_\mathrm{eff}$ is, up to a constant, the exchange $J\,\mathbf{S}\cdot\mathbf{s}$ between the impurity spin and the spin at the Fermi level, with $J = 2V^2 \left(1/|\varepsilon_d| + 1/(\varepsilon_d + U)\right)$.

An impurity with 81 bath levels

The full space has dimension $2^{2N + 2}$, and the effective Hamiltonian is a $4 × 4$ matrix.

using FermionicHilbertSpaces, KrylovKit, LinearAlgebra@fermions d cN = 81εd, U, V = -1.0, 3.0, 0.05εk = range(-1, 1, length = N)kF = (N + 1) ÷ 2spins = [:↑, :↓]H = tensor_product(hilbert_space(d, spins), hilbert_space(c, [(k, σ) for k in 1:N for σ in spins]))H0 = sum(εd * d[σ]' * d[σ] for σ in spins) + U * d[:↑]' * d[:↑] * d[:↓]' * d[:↓] +    sum(εk[k] * c[k, σ]' * c[k, σ] for k in 1:N for σ in spins)Vhyb = sum(V * d[σ]' * c[k, σ] + hc for k in 1:N for σ in spins)vacuum = Ket("0"^(2N + 2), H)fermi_sea = prod(c[k, σ]' for k in 1:(kF - 1) for σ in spins) * vacuumlow_energy = [d[σ]' * c[kF, τ]' * fermi_sea for σ in spins for τ in spins]E0 = εd + 2sum(εk[1:(kF - 1)])(log2_dimension = log2(dim(H)), effective_dimension = length(low_energy))
(log2_dimension = 164.0, effective_dimension = 4)

For each low-energy state $p$ we solve $(E_0 - H_0) x = V|p⟩$ and take overlaps with $V|p'⟩$. The result is compared with a second-order formula, so a loose solver tolerance is enough.

xs = [linsolve(x -> E0 * x - H0 * x, Vhyb * p; rtol = 1.0e-6)[1] for p in low_energy]Heff = [dot(Vhyb * p, x) for p in low_energy, x in xs]λ = eigvals(Hermitian(Heff))J = 2V^2 * (1 / abs(εd) + 1 / (εd + U))@assert isapprox(λ[2] - λ[1], J; rtol = 1.0e-4)@assert isapprox(Heff[2, 3], J / 2; rtol = 1.0e-4)

Removing the constant and dividing by $J$ leaves $\mathbf{S}\cdot\mathbf{s}$ in the basis $|{\uparrow\uparrow}⟩, |{\uparrow\downarrow}⟩, |{\downarrow\uparrow}⟩, |{\downarrow\downarrow}⟩$ of impurity and Fermi-level spins: the singlet lies $J$ below the triplet.
