Schrieffer–Wolff Transformation of the Anderson Model

The Anderson impurity model describes a level d with Hubbard repulsion U, hybridized with a bath of free fermions c. When the level is singly occupied, second-order perturbation theory in the hybridization V gives an effective antiferromagnetic Kondo coupling $J\,\mathbf{S}\cdot\mathbf{s}$ between the impurity spin and the bath spin. We compute this effective Hamiltonian with linsolve acting directly on symbolic states, in a Hilbert space far too large to enumerate.

using FermionicHilbertSpaces, KrylovKit, LinearAlgebraimport KrylovKit.VectorInterface: inner

The bath has N spinful levels with energies between -1 and 1, the middle one at the Fermi level. With 164 modes, the Hilbert space has dimension $2^{164}$. No basis is ever enumerated.

@fermions d cN = 81εd, U, V = -1.0, 3.0, 0.05εk = range(-1, 1, length=N)kF = (N + 1) ÷ 2spins = [:↑, :↓]H = tensor_product(hilbert_space(d, spins), hilbert_space(c, [(k, σ) for k in 1:N for σ in spins]))H0 = sum(εd * d[σ]' * d[σ] for σ in spins) + U * d[:↑]' * d[:↑] * d[:↓]' * d[:↓] +     sum(εk[k] * c[k, σ]' * c[k, σ] for k in 1:N for σ in spins)Vhyb = sum(V * d[σ]' * c[k, σ] + hc for k in 1:N for σ in spins)

The low-energy states have one electron on the impurity, one electron at the Fermi level, and all levels below it filled. We build them by acting with creation operators on the empty state.

vacuum = Ket("0"^(2N + 2), H)fermi_sea = prod(c[k, σ]' for k in 1:kF-1 for σ in spins) * vacuumlow_energy = [d[σ]' * c[kF, τ]' * fermi_sea for σ in spins for τ in spins]E0 = εd + 2sum(εk[1:kF-1])

The second-order effective Hamiltonian is $H_\mathrm{eff} = P V (E_0 - H_0)^{-1} V P$. Vhyb changes the impurity occupation, so Vhyb * p has no overlap with the low-energy states and the inverse is well defined. For each low-energy state we solve $(E_0 - H_0) x = V|p⟩$ and take overlaps with $V|p'⟩$.

function effective_hamiltonian(basis, H0, V, E0)    xs = map(basis) do p        x, info = linsolve(x -> E0 * x - H0 * x, V * p)        x    end    return [inner(V * p, x) for p in basis, x in xs]endHeff = effective_hamiltonian(low_energy, H0, Vhyb, E0)

The four states split into a singlet and a triplet, plus a uniform shift from virtual processes involving the Fermi sea. The singlet–triplet splitting is the Kondo coupling, which Schrieffer and Wolff found to be $J = 2V^2 \left(1/|\varepsilon_d| + 1/(\varepsilon_d + U)\right)$.

λ = eigvals(Hermitian(Heff))J_SW = 2V^2 * (1 / abs(εd) + 1 / (εd + U))(splitting=λ[2] - λ[1], J_SW=J_SW)

The spin-flip matrix element between $d_\uparrow^\dagger c_{k_F\downarrow}^\dagger$ and $d_\downarrow^\dagger c_{k_F\uparrow}^\dagger$ is $J/2$, as for $J\,\mathbf{S}\cdot\mathbf{s}$.
